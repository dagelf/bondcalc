Methodology & FAQ

This page exists so every number in the calculator can be checked. If you find an error, it is arithmetic — point it out and it gets fixed.

The real estate leg

Monthly simulation over the chosen horizon (default 120 months). Inflows: rent, grown annually, reduced by vacancy. Outflows: bond repayment (standard amortization), rates & taxes, maintenance, insurance, optional levies, management fee, and income tax on net rental at your marginal rate. Bond interest (not the capital repayment) is treated as tax-deductible against rental income, matching SARS practice for a genuine let property.

Upfront capital = deposit + transfer duty + bond registration attorney fee + transfer attorney fee + bond initiation fee, all added at month 0.

At exit: property sold at the compounded growth rate, less agent commission (default 5% + VAT) and capital gains tax, less the outstanding bond balance.

The Bitcoin leg

Starts with the identical month-0 capital. Every month, it invests exactly the real estate leg's net shortfall (bond + costs − net rent) — nothing more, nothing less. If the property is cash-positive in a given month, the Bitcoin leg invests R0 that month, and the property leg's surplus is banked at the money-market rate rather than vanishing. This symmetry rule is deliberate: neither leg is allowed a compounding advantage the other doesn't get. A 0.1% exchange fee applies per purchase. At exit, capital gains tax is applied using the same inclusion-rate method as the property leg — no favourable treatment either direction.

The Bitcoin forward return is a slider you control. The default (20% p.a.) is set below Bitcoin's historical CAGR on purpose — the tool does not need Bitcoin to perform historically well to make its point. A 0% preset is provided specifically so the comparison survives with Bitcoin's future returns assumed to be nil.

The kicker row

"Even if Bitcoin returns 0%, this property needs ≥ X% p.a. price growth just to break even after all costs." X is solved numerically: it's the property price growth rate at which the property leg's own money-weighted return (XIRR) is exactly 0%. This calculation never references Bitcoin — it is pure carry-cost arithmetic, which is why it holds up even if you have no view on Bitcoin at all.

Frequently asked / objected

"You ignored the leverage benefit of gearing."
No — leverage is fully modeled. The bond, its interest, and its amortization are the largest single line items in the simulation. That's precisely what the carry-cost waterfall exposes: gearing cuts both ways, and the interest bill is usually the biggest number a buyer has never seen written down.
"Your Bitcoin default is unrealistic."
The default (20% p.a.) is set below Bitcoin's historical compound annual growth rate, and a 0% preset is one click away. The kicker row is true at 0% Bitcoin growth. If you think even 20% is too generous, set it to 0% and the property arithmetic still stands or falls on its own.
"Property has intangible benefits — you can live in it, borrow against it, control it."
True, and out of scope by design — this tool prices a buy-to-let investment, not a home. If you plan to live in the property, several assumptions (management fee, vacancy, income tax on rental) don't apply to you and should be zeroed out in the assumptions drawer.
"Your tax tables will go stale."
The effective date is stamped in the footer and this page. Data is refreshed manually on a monthly cadence via a small build script — see the source repository.
"This is financial advice."
It isn't. There is no recommendation anywhere in this tool — only arithmetic on assumptions you control. Speak to a licensed financial adviser before making a real decision.
